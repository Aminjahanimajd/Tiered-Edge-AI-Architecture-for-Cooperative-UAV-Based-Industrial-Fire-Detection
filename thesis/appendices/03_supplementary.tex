\chapter{Supplementary Analyses and Failure Chronology}
\label{app:supplementary}

This appendix preserves the negative and incomplete outcomes that constrain the
final interpretation. The chronology is analytical rather than exhaustive: raw
timestamps, command transcripts and hashes remain in the repository evidence.
A later successful attempt does not erase an earlier failure when that failure
explains a design change or a validity boundary.

\section{Supplementary interpretation of timing}

The timing layers in the evidence are intentionally non-interchangeable.
Laptop inference-call latency excludes device runtime effects. The historical
device backend benchmarks exclude camera capture, communication and final
decision handling. ROS~2/DDS RTT measures an application request and reply on
the Sentinel monotonic clock. Alert-to-final-decision latency includes the
Scout verification path for a correlated live request. A ratio between two
layers is reported only when its limited meaning is explicit.

Within the retained stationary trials, Scout inference dominated the final
decision delay, while the median communication RTT was much smaller. The
earlier 400.453~ms communication maximum remains relevant to timeout design,
and the absence of loaded or moving-network conditions prevents a broader
communication guarantee. Likewise, the short backend loops cannot establish
steady-state temperature, power or memory behaviour.

\section{Preservation and revision rule}

Raw observations remain immutable. Cleaning, grouping, metric calculation and
figure generation create new derived files with a recorded parent. Failed,
interrupted and rejected attempts remain distinguishable from accepted runs.
The evidence inventory records repository-relative path, byte size, UTC
modification time and SHA-256 so that later revisions can identify exactly
which artifact changed.

Results arriving after the cutoff may be incorporated only by revising every
affected layer: experiment register, method, result table or figure,
interpretation, abstract and conclusion. This rule prevents a later isolated
number from silently changing the answer to a research question.
